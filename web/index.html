05 / Product decisions in the history

From a user need to working behavior.

These stories describe existing work. They were organized for the public release; they are not claims that retrospective story text was written before development.

May 2026 · player and contract models

“Help me understand a player beyond a single score.”

Player models grew to carry roles, salary horizons, and optional contract fields. Missing values remain visible rather than becoming invented estimates.

May–June 2026 · trade constraints

“Tell me why a trade fails.”

The rule engine separates blocking findings from warnings, includes salary-matching tiers, and adds targeted tests for decision boundaries.

June 2026 · lineup exploration

“Let me inspect who fits together.”

Depth-chart and lineup work connects positional eligibility to a team view, with explanations and explicit limits on inference.

July–August 2026 · broader application

“Let me explore, compare, and try a scenario.”

The source history includes fantasy scoring, player comparisons, shot-profile views, and deterministic basketball games.

September 2026 · public offline edition

“Let me run this without an account.”

This browser workbench uses 24 invented players, local APIs, and deterministic reports. The repository also preserves the reviewed Swift application components and their development history.

The browser workbench is a runnable public edition. The retained Swift components document broader native work; they are not a turnkey build of the connected iOS client.
